# India accounts

For rupee accounts, such as ICICI and SBI. It keeps them apart from your pound budget and adds up their interest for your UK return. Not tax advice.

## Before you start

- **Update every iPhone and Mac that shares your budget first.** An older version can delete details it doesn't understand.
- Make a backup (Settings › Backup).

## Switch it on

**Settings › India Accounts.** On iPhone it opens from the row on Home; on the Mac from the sidebar. Switching it off hides the screens and keeps your details.

## Add your accounts

**Add Account:** the bank, whether it's NRE, NRO, resident savings or a fixed deposit, the last four digits, and whose it is. A joint account's interest is split equally. Mark it **Private** to keep it and its lines from your partner. For an account without statements, type its balance.

## Import a statement

1. In your bank's website or app, download the account statement for the dates you want, ideally from 6 April.
2. The app reads text files: **CSV** or tab-separated text (some downloads of this kind have names ending .xls). For a real Excel file, open it in Numbers or Excel and export it as CSV. It can't read a PDF.
3. **Import Statement**, choose the file and the account, check the preview, then import.

The columns are found by name: a date, the description, withdrawal and deposit (or debit and credit), and the balance. If the file isn't recognised, nothing is imported.

Importing overlapping statements adds nothing twice. Downloads with a balance column are matched most reliably. A warning that payments don't add up to the balance means a line is missing.

Import rupee statements here, not under Activity, or they would be counted as pounds.

## Balances in pounds

**Exchange Rates:** type the rupees to one pound for today's balances, and an average for each tax year. The app doesn't look rates up. HMRC publishes yearly averages on GOV.UK.

## Interest for your UK return

**Interest for UK Tax** shows, for each person and tax year (6 April to 5 April), the interest and the tax deducted in India, in rupees and pounds, with an export for an accountant.

- Interest and tax are picked out from the bank's wording. Open a line to correct it.
- Add deposit interest from the bank's certificate with **Add Interest or Tax**. Don't type in what a statement already has.
- Banks may pay interest after taking tax off. The UK return needs the interest before tax, so compare with the interest certificate or Form 26AS.
- UK residents are normally taxed on interest wherever it arises, including NRE interest that India doesn't tax; some recent arrivals can claim relief.
- Relief for Indian tax is limited to the rate in the India–UK treaty, which can be less than the bank deducted.

## What isn't covered

These figures aren't part of the Property & Tax estimate. Nor are mutual funds, shares, capital gains, rent from property in India or your Indian tax return.
