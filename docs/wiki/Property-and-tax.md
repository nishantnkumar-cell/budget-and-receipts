# Property and tax

For UK rental property and Self Assessment. It uses the payments already in Duo Budget, so rent and costs aren't typed twice. It prepares figures for you to check; you send the return yourself. Estimates only, not tax advice.

## Before you start

- **Update every iPhone and Mac that shares your budget first.** An older version can delete tax details it doesn't understand.
- Make a backup (Settings › Backup).
- Your budget must be in pounds.
- Import statements from 6 April at the start of the tax year.

## Switch it on

**Settings › Property & Tax.** On iPhone it opens from the row on Home; on the Mac from the sidebar. Switching it off hides the screens and keeps your details.

## Set up

1. **Properties and Rules:** add each property and your share of it. A married couple's joint property is taxed half each unless HMRC has a Form 17.
2. **Property Payments:** choose a payment, its property and what kind of money it is. Turn on **Always treat … this way** to make a rule. For a split payment, choose each part. You can also do this from the payment's own screen.
3. **Pay and Statements:** enter each P60, any letting agent statements, the lender's interest paid between 6 April and 5 April, losses brought forward, payments on account already made, Gift Aid, your UK bank interest and dividends outside ISAs, and your pension saving.

**Pension annual allowance:** enter everything paid in for the year, the part given up by salary sacrifice, and unused allowance from the three earlier years. The estimate reduces the £60,000 allowance for high incomes, adds any charge to your bill, and takes off what your scheme pays. Check the result against your scheme's savings statement. The lower allowance after flexibly taking benefits isn't covered.

Interest and dividends in [India accounts](India-accounts) are added to the estimate at your rate for the year, with relief for Indian tax: no more than 15% on interest and 10% on dividends, and no more than the UK tax that income causes.

## Import agent statements and payslips

Both are in **Pay and Statements**. Files are read on your device, and you see what was read before anything is saved. If a file isn't recognised, nothing is imported: add it by hand.

**Agent statements:** PDFs headed “Remittance Advice” or “Management Statement”, or a CSV with columns for property, gross rent, management fee, deductions, net payment and date, in that order. Import your bank statement first: each agent statement is then linked to the payment of the same amount, so the rent isn't counted twice. A remittance advice is checked against the amount it says was paid; a management statement shows nothing to check against, so compare it yourself. Passwords must be removed first.

**Payslips:** PDF payslips showing “Total Taxable Pay”, “PAYE Net Pay” and figures to date. The latest payslip in each tax year sets that year's taxable pay and tax deducted for the employer name you give; figures you typed in, such as from a P60, are only replaced if you switch that on. Choose files holding one payslip each. Compare both figures with the payslip before saving.

## How nothing is counted twice

- An agent statement gives the rent before fees and replaces the bank payment you link it to.
- Mortgage payments aren't counted; the lender's yearly interest figure is.
- Buying or improving a property isn't counted as an expense.

## Read the results

**Not ready to file** lists anything to sort out, such as missing statements or a payment without a category. **Return Figures** shows the UK property page boxes for each person. **Whose Name to Buy In?** compares the extra income tax on a new property.

## Copies for HMRC

In **Return Figures**, choose whose return, for **2025-26**:

- **Box-by-Box Sheet (PDF):** every form and box with its figure, in whole pounds, with what to check. Use it to type the return into HMRC's online service, or give it to an accountant.
- **Fill HMRC Forms:** download the blank forms it lists from GOV.UK, choose them, and save one PDF with your figures on HMRC's own pages (SA100, SA101 if there's a pension charge, SA102 for each job, SA105 and SA106). Print at actual size.

**Deadlines:** HMRC had to receive paper returns for 2025-26 by 31 October 2026. Posting one now means a late filing penalty unless HMRC's letter to you gave a later date. Online returns are due by 31 January 2027. HMRC's online service can't take a PDF.

Your name, tax reference, National Insurance number, signature and anything the app doesn't hold are for you to add. Pages are marked **Not ready to file** while something needs sorting out: for example the same job entered twice, pay that only comes from a payslip earlier than the year's end (check it against your P60 or P45 and save it by hand), or, in a shared household, your partner's return made on your device. Make each person's forms on their own device. The property income allowance isn't applied. Interest from India Accounts goes on the foreign pages (or, if untaxed, small and your only foreign income, one box of the main return), with tax relief limited to 15%. The files aren't encrypted.

## Sharing with your partner

Property details are shared. Pay is private unless you turn on **Show to** your partner, and only the person who typed it in can change it. Choices about payments you keep private stay private, so your figures may then differ from your partner's.

## What isn't covered

Capital gains, other income from abroad, Scottish rates, student loans, Child Benefit charge, the property income allowance, accruals accounting, limits on interest where loans exceed the property's value, stamp duty, and tax years from 2027-28.

Figures use the cash basis: money counts when it's received or paid. Rent a letting agent holds counts when the agent receives it, so check statements dated around 5 April. If your rent is high enough for **Making Tax Digital**, the app says so; it doesn't send quarterly updates.
